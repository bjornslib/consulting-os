# Stakeholder Alignment

Recommendations are not approved in meetings; they are approved before meetings. This skill maps who actually shapes the decision, understands what each person needs (which is rarely what they say), and sequences honest one-on-one work so the decision meeting confirms rather than decides.

## Method

**1. Map the real decision network.** For the specific decision: the formal approver, the people whose objection can kill it regardless of the org chart, the influencers the approver privately consults, the implementers whose quiet non-cooperation can starve it later, and those affected who deserve a voice. Titles mislead; ask "whose no matters, whose yes moves others?"

**2. Assess each stakeholder honestly.**
- **Position**: champion / supportive / neutral / sceptical / opposed — based on evidence (what they have said and done), marked as inference where it is inference.
- **Interest**: what they gain or lose — organisationally (budget, headcount, scope) and personally (reputation, workload, autonomy). The stated objection is often not the operative one; note both, and treat the personal layer with respect rather than cynicism.
- **Influence**: over this decision specifically.
- **What movement requires**: evidence, a concession, a role in shaping it, air cover, or simply being asked early.

**3. Prioritise the campaign.** High-influence sceptics and opposed stakeholders first — surfacing their objections early is a gift, since objections discovered in the decision meeting are fatal. Champions get ammunition and a role; neutrals with high influence get personal briefings before the group sees anything.

**4. Run the pre-wire sequence.** One-on-ones in deliberate order (usually: friendly-but-honest critic first to pressure-test, then sceptics, then the approver last with "here is what your team thinks and how the concerns were addressed"). In each: show the thinking early enough that they can genuinely change it; incorporate what improves the answer; be explicit about what was raised and not adopted, and why. Adjusting the recommendation on merit is the mechanism, not a defeat.

**5. Track and maintain.** Positions shift; the map is refreshed after every significant interaction. Post-decision, the implementer group becomes the priority — alignment decays fastest where the work lands.

**Ethical line**: this is transparency-tested influence. If a stakeholder would feel deceived on learning how they were approached, redesign the approach.
