# Profit-Pool Analysis

Revenue tells you where activity is; profit tells you where power is. This skill maps the total profit in an industry or value chain, shows who captures it and why, and identifies where it is migrating — which is where strategy should point.

## Method

**1. Define the pool's boundary.** Which value chain, which geography, which period. Use the market definition from sizing work if it exists.

**2. Decompose the value chain into activities** (e.g. make → distribute → sell → service → finance) or into segments/products if that is the sharper cut. Choose the cut that exposes the client's real strategic question.

**3. Estimate revenue and margin per link.**
- Revenue per link: from market sizing, filings, and industry data.
- Margin per link: triangulate from pure-play players' filings (the cleanest signal), client economics, and expert input. Where players span links, allocate with stated logic.
- Multiply to get absolute profit per link. Absolute dollars, not just percentages — a fat margin on a thin sliver can matter less than a thin margin on a torrent.

**4. Draw the pool.** The classic chart: x-axis = share of industry revenue per link, y-axis = operating margin per link; area = profit. It makes the imbalance visible in one image.

**5. Explain the shape.** For each fat pool: what protects it (scarcity, switching costs, regulation, scale, brand)? For each thin one: what competed it away? Barriers explain persistence.

**6. Find the migration.** Where is profit moving — driven by technology, regulation, channel shift, or customer behaviour? Historic shift plus current drivers gives the forward view. The strategic prize is usually in the migration, not the current map.
