# Operating-Model Design

Strategy chooses what to win; the operating model determines whether the organisation can. This skill designs the model backwards from the strategy's few "must-win" capabilities, rather than forwards from the org chart — because boxes are the last decision, not the first.

## Method

**1. Extract the design criteria from the strategy.** What must this organisation be distinctively good at for the chosen strategy to work (3–5 capabilities)? What must it deliberately *not* optimise for? Add hard constraints: regulatory, geographic, cost envelope. These criteria arbitrate every later design argument.

**2. Design across six layers, in this order:**
- **Value streams / processes** — how value actually flows to customers; where the critical hand-offs are.
- **Capabilities** — what must be built, bought, borrowed, or automated to run those streams. For AI-era models: which decisions and tasks move to systems, which stay human, and how the human–machine seam is managed.
- **Structure** — grouping logic (function, product, customer, geography) chosen to make the *most important* collaboration cheap; every grouping choice taxes some other collaboration, so name the tax and its mitigation.
- **Governance and decision rights** — who decides what, with what money, at what speed. Use a decision-rights table for the 10–15 decisions that matter most; ambiguity here is where models rot.
- **Technology and data** — the systems and data flows the model assumes; flag where current systems cannot support the design.
- **People and ways of working** — roles that change, capacity and skills gaps, incentives, and the behaviours the model needs (which incentives must reinforce, or they won't happen).

**3. Test the design before drawing final boxes.** Walk 3–5 concrete scenarios through it ("a major client escalates", "a new AI product needs launching in 6 weeks") and check the model handles them without heroics. Redesign where it snags.

**4. Size the transition.** Current → target gap per layer; sequence (usually: decision rights and processes before structure — moving boxes first creates chaos with no capability gain); cost and disruption estimate.

Before drafting the final deliverable, load `references/deliverable.md` for the output template and quality bar.
