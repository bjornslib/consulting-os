# Executive Narrative & Decks

Executives do not read decks; they audit them. The narrative must survive the reader who only reads titles, the reader who attacks page 7, and the reader who asks "so what" after the first minute. This skill builds the storyline first and pages second — never the reverse.

## Method

**1. Fix the governing thought.** One sentence: the answer to the governing question, specific enough to disagree with. If the engagement did its job, this is the recommendation from the options work. Everything in the document exists to support this sentence.

**2. Build the pyramid.** Under the governing thought, 3–5 key-line arguments that together make it undeniable — grouped MECE, ordered by the logic the audience needs (deductive for sceptical audiences who must be walked to the conclusion; grouping/inductive for aligned audiences who want the support surveyed). Each key line decomposes into the evidence beneath it. Test: reading only the governing thought and key lines should convey the entire argument.

**3. Write the storyline as action titles before making any page.** The "ghost deck": every page is one full-sentence title that asserts something ("SME churn concentrates in the first 90 days and is driven by onboarding failure"), never a label ("Churn analysis"). Read the titles top to bottom — they must flow as a self-sufficient argument. Fix the storyline at this stage; it is ten times cheaper than fixing built pages.

**4. Design each page as one claim + its proof.** The title asserts; the body proves — one exhibit or one structured argument per page, with the source noted. If a page needs two exhibits to make its point, it is two pages or the point is unclear. Kill pages whose title the argument does not need; move interesting-but-unnecessary material to appendix.

**5. Open with the answer.** Executive summary first: situation–complication in two lines, then the recommendation and the key lines. Suspense is for novels; in the boardroom, the reader who knows the answer reads the support more charitably.

**6. Pressure-test before release.** The three audits: titles-only read-through (does the argument stand?); hostile-reader pass (which page would the biggest sceptic attack — is it reinforced?); and "so what" per page (every page earns its place by advancing the argument).
