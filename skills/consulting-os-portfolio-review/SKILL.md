# Portfolio Review

Portfolios drift: yesterday's bets keep their budgets by incumbency. A portfolio review re-earns every position by asking three questions of each unit — is it attractive, are we advantaged, and does it fit — and then forces allocation decisions that sum to the available capital.

## Method

**1. Define the units of review.** Business units, product lines, client accounts, or initiatives — whatever the allocation decision actually moves. Get the economics per unit clean first (fully-loaded, with shared costs allocated on stated logic; unallocated "corporate" pools hide the truth).

**2. Assess each unit on two axes plus a fit test.**
- **Market attractiveness**: size, growth, profit-pool position, structural trend.
- **Competitive position**: share, relative economics, differentiation durability.
- **Ownership fit**: is the client the best owner of this asset — do they add value to it that another owner couldn't? A good business can still be a divestment if someone else would pay more than it is worth inside.

**3. Plot and classify.** The grid (attractiveness × position) suggests postures: invest/grow, hold/harvest, fix (with a dated fix-or-exit condition), exit. Treat the grid as a conversation-forcing device, not an oracle — the interesting arguments are about units near boundaries.

**4. Test the portfolio as a system.** Cash balance (do harvest units fund growth units?), risk concentration, capability sharing, and strategic coherence — does the set tell one story?

**5. Force the allocation.** Translate postures into next-cycle capital and talent allocation that sums to what exists. Every "fix" gets a deadline and a condition; every "exit" gets a path (sell, wind down, merge) and a value expectation.
