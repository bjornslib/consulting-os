# Market Sizing

A market size is only as credible as the method behind it. This skill produces a number a sceptical CFO can interrogate: defined boundaries, two independent methods that converge, and stated sensitivity.

## Method

**1. Define the market before sizing it.** State explicitly: product/service boundary, customer boundary, geographic boundary, time period, and unit (revenue, units, seats, GMV). Most sizing disputes are definition disputes in disguise.

**2. Size top-down.** Start from a credible anchor (industry total, category spend, adjacent published market) and apply explicit filters to narrow to the defined market. Every filter is a named percentage with a source or stated logic.

**3. Size bottom-up.** Build from units: number of potential buyers × penetration or purchase frequency × price. Prefer client or primary data for at least one factor. Bottom-up is the credibility engine; top-down is the sanity check.

**4. Triangulate.** If the two methods land within ~±25% of each other, present the range. If they diverge more, do not average — find which input drives the gap and resolve it. The reconciliation is often the most valuable insight in the exercise.

**5. Layer TAM → SAM → SOM.** TAM uses the market definition; SAM applies real constraints (geography served, channel access, regulatory reach, segment fit); SOM applies competitive reality (achievable share given competitors, sales capacity, ramp time). SOM must be justified by a share-capture logic, never a "conservative 1%".

**6. Project growth from drivers, not trend lines.** Decompose growth into volume drivers and price drivers; state which are structural and which cyclical.
