# Competitive Intelligence

The goal is not a directory of competitors; it is a predictive model of each one that matters: their economics, their strategy as revealed by behaviour, and their likely moves.

## Method

**1. Map the field before profiling anyone.** Identify direct competitors, indirect substitutes, and credible entrants (adjacent players with the assets to enter). Group into strategic clusters by how they compete (cost, differentiation, niche, platform), not just what they sell.

**2. Profile the 3–6 that matter.** For each:
- **Facts**: size, growth, ownership, footprint, offer, pricing where observable.
- **Economics**: revealed cost structure and margin logic — infer from filings, pricing behaviour, staffing patterns.
- **Strategy revealed by behaviour**: what they invest in, hire for, acquire, and exit. Actions over press releases.
- **Vulnerabilities**: segments underserved, structural cost disadvantages, dependencies (one channel, one customer, legacy tech), organisational constraints.
- **Likely next moves**: given their economics and stated ambitions, what would a rational version of them do?

**3. Benchmark on dimensions the customer values**, weighted by segment. A feature table nobody buys on is decoration.

**4. Separate signal from noise.** Date every data point; mark rumours as rumours. Intelligence has a shelf life — state it.

**Sources — lawful and ethical only**: filings, pricing pages, job adverts (a hiring pattern is a strategy leak), patents, conference talks, customer reviews, win/loss interviews, ex-employee interviews conducted transparently and within confidentiality obligations. Never misrepresentation, pretexting, or soliciting confidential material.
