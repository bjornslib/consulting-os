# Transformation Roadmaps

A roadmap is a commitment device, not a picture. Every element on it needs an owner, a date, a definition of done, and a funding source — otherwise it is a mural. This skill builds roadmaps that survive contact with the organisation.

## Method

**1. Set the destination and the proof points.** Restate the target state (from strategy and operating-model work) and define 3–5 observable proof points that would demonstrate the transformation is working — these anchor the milestone design.

**2. Structure into waves, not just dates.** Each wave has a theme and an exit criterion — the demonstrable condition that permits the next wave (e.g. "Wave 1 exits when the new pricing runs live in one region with realised price within 3% of model"). Waves gated on results beat waves gated on calendar.

**3. Map dependencies ruthlessly.** Hard dependencies (technical, regulatory, contractual) versus soft (preference, convenience). The critical path runs through hard dependencies; challenge every soft one that sits on it. Identify the single longest-lead item on day one — it usually needs starting before the plan is even approved.

**4. Assign single-name ownership and real funding.** Every workstream: one accountable owner (a name, not a committee), a funded budget with its source, and the decision forum it reports into. Milestones each get a definition of done that a sceptic could verify.

**5. Plan the change, not just the build.** For each wave: who is affected, what they lose and gain, the communication and training load, and the change-absorption check against everything else those teams are being asked to do. Transformations mostly fail here, not in the build.

**6. Build the steering rhythm.** Cadence of review, the dashboard of leading indicators (from KPI work), thresholds that trigger escalation, and the explicit re-planning mechanism — a roadmap without a re-planning routine becomes fiction by month three.
